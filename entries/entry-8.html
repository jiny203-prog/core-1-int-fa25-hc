False Morning

The alarm rings at seven, but I’ve been awake since five. I never really slept—just drifted in and out of thoughts that didn’t go anywhere. My eyes feel heavy but my mind keeps working, like a machine that forgot how to turn off.

The sunlight is already in the room. It looks warm, but it feels fake, like a bright filter over something tired. I get up and go through the usual motions—wash my face, open the blinds, check my phone. Every step feels rehearsed. I can’t remember the last time I actually felt rested while doing this.

Outside, the city sounds normal. Cars pass, people walk, coffee machines start to hum. Everything moves at its usual rhythm, except me. My thoughts are still slow, half stuck in the night. It’s like I woke up in someone else’s morning.

I make coffee, but it tastes wrong—too bitter, too early. I sit there and scroll, waiting for my body to realize that it’s a new day. The light hurts my eyes, but I keep staring at it anyway, trying to convince myself that being awake means I’m alive.

But it doesn’t. Not yet.

False morning feels like pretending. Pretending that I slept. Pretending that I’m ready. Pretending that the night is over when it’s really still inside me. Some days begin, but they don’t really start—and I carry the darkness quietly into the light.
